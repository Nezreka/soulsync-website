MetaSync

The network layer for SoulSync

SoulSync works completely on its own. MetaSync is what SoulSync installs can choose to plug into: two shared networks that make every connected library smarter, while no install ever gives up control of its own data.

Two networks, one companion

What MetaSync Is

MetaSync is a small companion program that runs next to your SoulSync, with its own interface on its own port. It speaks to SoulSync only through SoulSync's API, and it is the bridge to two opt-in networks.

The Metadata Pool

Every SoulSync install spends real work resolving metadata: matching tracks, albums and artists across providers. MetaSync lets installs pool that resolved metadata, keyed by the SoulID every track, album and artist already gets in SoulSync, so one install's work does not have to be redone by every other install. Records are checked against other installs before they are trusted, and your own library's data always wins over the pool's. The pool carries metadata only. Never audio, never your files.

SIN: The SoulSync Intelligence Network

SIN pools anonymous taste signals from connected installs: which artists get watchlisted, which albums get wishlisted, land in libraries, get downloaded and kept, and which releases people are still hunting for. Those counts are combined across the whole network and turned into things your SoulSync can use: Trending, Most Wanted, Just Landed, Loved & Kept, Rising and Breakout lists. SoulSync picks them up from MetaSync once a day, matches every track, and shows them as SoulSync Charts on its dashboard and Discover page: open one and it is a ready playlist, and nothing downloads until you press Sync & download. Signals are counted once per install per item per week, and a release only appears once at least 15 installs stand behind it.

Yours to Run, Yours to Leave

MetaSync is free and open source, like SoulSync. Run it as a Python program on the same machine as SoulSync, or as a Docker container next to it. SoulSync itself never depends on it: stop MetaSync, unplug it, or never install it, and SoulSync runs exactly as before. Joining a network is a separate step inside MetaSync, never a side effect of installing it.

Getting connected

How to Connect

Three steps. The first connects MetaSync to your SoulSync. Only the third one joins a network.

STEP 1

Install MetaSync and connect it to your SoulSync

Run MetaSync next to your SoulSync and open its interface. In SoulSync, go to Settings, Advanced, MetaSync and press Connect: SoulSync finds MetaSync and shows a pairing code to approve on the MetaSync dashboard, and the two swap keys. Typing the keys by hand works too. MetaSync can now read your library through the API, and create playlists in it. Nothing has left your machine.

STEP 2

Get a pool key

Sign in at the MetaSync portal, metasync.ssync.net, and create a key. It is shown once; the portal keeps only its fingerprint. That key is how the pool knows a real account is connecting. It can be regenerated or revoked at any time, and revoking it cuts that install off on its very next request without affecting anyone else.

STEP 3

Join the pool, and approve your MetaSync

Paste your key into SoulSync's MetaSync section (or MetaSync's Pools page). The first time MetaSync presents it, the portal asks you to approve that MetaSync by its fingerprint: from then on the key works only from that one install, so a copied key is useless anywhere else. MetaSync starts in dry run and shows you the exact report it would send, so you can read every line yourself. Turn dry run off and it sends one report a week and keeps the network's charts ready for your SoulSync to show.

Privacy

What Leaves Your Machine, and What Never Does

SIN runs on counts, not on your data. This is the whole list of what a weekly SIN report contains, for the week that just ended: MusicBrainz IDs for artists you watchlisted, and releases you wishlisted, added, downloaded, kept for two weeks, or are still hunting for, each counted once. Two more are optional and off until you switch them on: albums you downloaded and later removed, and albums you played at least three different tracks from (your top 50 at most). A report is a week of events, never your library: a week with hundreds of imports is a migration, and is held back rather than sent.

Never Collected

No listening history: the optional listening signal is a yes or no per album per week, with no play counts, no play times and no track names, and nothing from a SoulSync profile that keeps its own listening history. No file paths or library listings. No artist or album names as text, only IDs. No usernames, and no tracking of who reported what: your key checks you in at the door, and the report is counted the moment it arrives and never stored. What the pool keeps is a tally per album, not your report.

Preview Before It Sends

MetaSync shows you the exact report before the first send, and the latest report is always one click away in its interface. If you do not like what you see, do not join the pool, or leave it. Leaving takes effect immediately; the pool keeps only the anonymous counts already combined into past weeks.

Only Official Playlists Are Official

The global playlists everyone receives are published by the network itself, from the combined counts. Individual installs can only send their own signals and pull the finished playlists down. Nobody can edit the shared lists, inject tracks into them, or publish on anyone else's behalf.

Status

Where This Stands

MetaSync and SIN are in public beta. The SIN pool and the account portal are live, and the first installs are reporting. A list only publishes once at least 15 installs report the same release in a week, so right now the network is gathering its founding installs: early testers are exactly what it needs. The rules above are on record here, and the full data map is in the MetaSync repository.
